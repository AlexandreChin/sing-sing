# Guided carousel run

The author decides, you carry. Never pick an option for them, never skip a step, never rewrite an option yourself unless they give you the text. One step per message.

## 1. Start

```bash
python main.py produce <input.txt> --guided [--no-api] [--medium article|video|podcast] [--title "…"] [--source "…"] [--url …] [--published-at "…"] [--language <langue>]
```

Pass the source metadata flags the author gave you (they fill the slide-1 pill). Add `--no-api` when the run goes through the local `claude` CLI.

The run pauses at the first step with no pick (steps 1–7: question, enjeu, idees, moments, contexte, socle, hook; `writing` runs without stopping). stdout holds the options, stderr the line `Awaiting a choice: python main.py choose <fmt_dir> <step> --pick <ids>` — use that exact `<fmt_dir>` and `<step>`.

## 2. Relay the options

Show the author the options of the paused step in chat, faithfully: each option's id, its text, its reason (↳), its flags (⚠) and which one is ★ recommended. Do not summarise, merge or reorder. Say which step it is (n of 7) and ask for their choice. Stop there and wait.

## 3. Map the reply to a command

| Author says | Run |
|---|---|
| « go with b » / « b et d » | `python main.py choose <fmt_dir> <step> --pick b` (comma-separated ids when the step keeps several) |
| a rewrite of an option | `… --pick b --edit "b=<their text>"` (`b.field=…` for another field; `--edit` is repeatable) |
| « other suggestions », with or without a remark | `… --more [--remark "<their words>"]` — prints new options; relay them and wait again |

When the author gives a reason, pass it verbatim with `--reason "…"`; it goes in the choice log. Never invent one. Relay any ⚠ warning `choose` prints, and propose a fix for each (a rewritten text); apply it only once the author accepts — `choose … --edit ID=TEXT` again, or `fix` for a deck field.

Then rerun `produce <input.txt> --guided …` (same flags) to reach the next pause. Repeat until no step is awaiting. `produce` renders only with `--render`: after the last pick (step 7), the final rerun is `produce <input.txt> --guided --render` (an unattended run needs `--render` too for slides). A rerun right after `choose --more` re-prints the same pending step with the new options — expected, not a loop. `--no-api` goes on `choose --more` too.

## 4. After the render

```bash
python main.py check <fmt_dir>/extract.json --reader [--no-api]
```

Hard-check errors must be fixed; « editorial (advisory) » flags are for the author to weigh. Show each finding to the author with a proposed fix (a field path and new text). Apply only the accepted ones:

```bash
python main.py fix <fmt_dir> <field> "<text>"     # e.g. hook.subtitle or display.reading_beats[0].answer
```

`fix` edits the field and re-stamps the steps without regenerating. Then `python main.py produce <input.txt> --guided --render` to re-render, and show the slide PNGs (`<fmt_dir>/slides/`).

## Going back

To change an earlier step, run a new `choose` on it (`--pick`, `--edit` or `--more`), then rerun `produce --guided`: the later steps are rebuilt from the new choice. Warn the author that their later picks on the rebuilt steps are asked again.

## The log

Every choice, edit and reason lands in `<fmt_dir>/choices.jsonl`, which also holds the `more` and `fix` events (a `fix` is a promotable correction). When a correction there looks reusable (a rewrite that repeats a pattern), **propose** to the author promoting it to `agent/prompts/carousel_steps/examples/<n>_<step>.md`, in that file's format: a `## Retenus` section and a `## Écartés (et pourquoi)` section. Do it only if they agree; never automatically.

## Unattended

`produce <input.txt>` without `--guided` takes the ★ recommended option at every step. Use it only if the author asks for a run without choosing.

## The CADRAGE & LOGIQUE archetype

For an opinion piece, essay or expert interview the author may ask for the `cadrage_logique` format (`--format cadrage_logique` on `produce`). The loop is the same — relay options, map the reply, `choose`, rerun — with seven steps: `axe` first (the arbitrage the deck leads to: `· variable`, `· position1`, `· position2`, `· thesis`; never rendered), then in slide order `constat`, `these`, `angle_mort`, `depassement`, `boussole`, `hook` (each keeps ONE option; ids `a`, `b`, …); `writing` (the caption) runs without a pause. An option shows its main text, then its other fields (`· fig1`, `· these_claim`, `· note1`, `· lever1`, `· question1`, `· pro1`…), then its reason (↳). A figure is `valeur | libellé | comparaison`, a note `source | texte` or `remarque | texte`, a lever `titre | détail`: keep that shape when the author rewrites one. `--edit` takes `ID=TEXT` or `ID.field=TEXT`. `fix` fields are `<step>.<field>` (`axe.position1`, `constat.fig1`, `these.solution_claim`, `angle_mort.title`, `boussole.con2`, `hook.promise`…) and `caption`. Slides 4 to 6 rest on the model's general knowledge, not on the source: tell the author the sources there are unverified pointers. `check` does not cover this format: the ⚠ that `choose` prints are its checks (a word cap, a malformed figure, note or lever, an accusing word, a figure not found in the source) — relay them and propose a fix as usual.

When the perspective of time is particularly helpful to analyse a position (the media asserts a novelty, a trend, a drift or a return that dated reference points can test), the author may ask for the `temps_long` format (`--format temps_long` on `produce`). Same loop, with seven steps: `axe` first (never rendered: `· fait` — the hook's title, the raw attributed fact, ≤64 characters, quotes verbatim, one `**key word**` in gold —, `· title` — the question the deck tests, ending in « ? » and taking a quoted word of the media —, `· move` — `forme`, `seuil`, `sequence`, `nouveaute`, `delai` or `memoire`, one per option when the article allows —, `· mot` — found in the source —, `· reponse`, `· criteres` — « Rupture, Continuité, Cycle » for `forme`, three names of ≤10 characters for `seuil`, empty otherwise; its promise must be keepable with dates, texts, attributed positions or tests, never a trend figure; the move can set the slide order, `seuil` puts the answer before the timelines), then `position` (the author's stand shown as slide 2, in the article's own words: `· title` — the claim, ≤46 characters, two lines, with at least one `**key passage**` —, `· lead` — the sentence under it, ≤90 characters, naming the author and saying who he is —, `· ecarte`, `· diagnostic`, `· mecanisme`, `· issue`; the `pieges` step has no `lead` any more: its `title` is the slide's only header), then `tournants` (the laws, doctrine texts and public statements that changed the rules over 30–50 years; `· lead`, `· rep1`…`· rep4`, the latest from the last three years — a bill under discussion included, with its status; its remark gives what it could change for the right to demonstrate, each thesis attributed to its camp), then `episodes` (the events of the last ten years people remember most, each with a documented trace), `lecture` (`· lead`, `· card1`…`· card3` — `nom | question ? | texte`, name ≤10 and equal to the axe's `criteres` when it has some, question ≤36 ending in « ? », text ≤100 with one **bold** passage —, `· where` and `· where_label`, `· kicker`, `· author`, `· question`; for `seuil`, a decision table instead: `· head1` / `· head2` (≤20 characters, the pair of words the first camp uses itself: « Approprié », « Excessif » — not the hook title's words unless the camp uses them); the rows `· card1`…`· card3` as `critère | question | première issue | seconde issue` (the question ≤28 characters says what the test looks at as a complement — « Qui est visé ? », « Par rapport aux violences ? », never « Quel effet ? » —, each outcome ≤30 characters in the camp's own words); `· aside_heading` (≤50, a paragraph that is the lead's twin — « Pour Amnesty, sept signes d'un « glissement ». ») and `· aside1`…`· aside7` (2 to 7) as `nom | phrase | icône` — a short name ≤10 characters that is also the timeline pill, the explicit phrase ≤34, an optional icon key (`book`, `speech_bubble`, `eye`, `flame`, `pushpin`, `hierarchy`, `shield`…); the lead ≤100, and `· where` / `· author` left empty. On `axe`, `· criteres2` lists the sign names, and a timeline point may carry up to 2 tags from `criteres` and `criteres2`, the step's `· key` (≤100 characters, under the rows) being optional and empty by default; for `forme` the lead reads « Pour trancher : comparer … d'un épisode à l'autre » and the cards are Rupture, Continuité, Cycle; an older deck's `· rupture`, `· continuite`, `· cycle` still work), `pieges` (`· piege1`…`· piege4`; no trap is imposed), `hook` (`· promise` — the axe's question, `· tagline` — one line, ≤56 characters, key words in `**gold**`),; `writing` runs without a pause. A reference point is `date | repère | remarque [| critère1, critère2]` (the optional fourth part tags the axe's criteria the point illustrates — only when its remark supports it; the date holds a year; the remark is one line saying what happened or what the text changed, different on every row and never repeating the label's words or the lead's quoted word; `episodes` takes 4 or 5 points), a reading `hypothèse ? | test` (with the shared `· where` line under the lead — an organisation, ≤48 characters, never a URL — the third part is optional) (the hypothesis is a question ending in « ? », the test holds a **bold** passage — what tells the reading apart: the gap, the direction, the return — drawn from slides 3–4, the third part names an organisation; the measure itself is in the lead, at the widest level the media claims — an age or place subgroup is a breakdown, not the measure), a trap `titre | question` (four of them): keep that shape when the author rewrites one. `fix` fields are `<step>.<field>` (`episodes.rep1`, `lecture.card3`, `pieges.piege1`, `axe.fait` — the hook's title —, `hook.promise`…) and `caption`. The dates and the organisations rest on the model's general knowledge, not on the source: tell the author to verify each date, and that « remembered most » is the model's judgement, made checkable only by the documented trace. No figure of any trend is allowed (a `%` or an amount comes back as ⚠). The `lead` of the two timelines must quote a word of the media; if the source makes no temporal claim, say that `temps_long` is a poor fit. Every text field has a character cap that keeps it within its lines: relay the ⚠ that `choose` prints rather than trimming silently. `check` does not cover this format.

For a striking figure (a statistic, a poll, a ranking) the author may ask for the `anatomie_chiffre` format (`--format anatomie_chiffre` on `produce`). Same loop, with six steps: `axe` first (never rendered as such: `· fait` — the movement to explain —, `· article` — `nom | formule`, the article's own reading, not drawn on slide 5 — and `· lecture1`, `· lecture2`, `· lecture3`, each `nom | formule`: three alternatives to it, each explaining the *rise*, never a mere measurement problem), then in slide order `chiffres` (the figures the article gives: `· claim` — what is measured (who, where, when), not the movement —, `· fig1`…`· fig4`, `· note1`; `fig1` is the key figure, drawn big and showing the movement « avant → après » (« 9 → 15 ») when the article gives both, its label a plain sentence that reads alone; `fig4` is an optional overall reference figure, drawn small; `note1` a remark glossing any statistical term such as « médian », shown under the key figure), `recit` (`· title` — the measured fact, describing the key figure — and `· row1`…`· row3`, each `type | texte | remarque` with a distinct type among `explication`, `interpretation`, `consequence`; the remark says the statement's status and what it depends on, never a shortfall), `angle_mort` (two blind spots of these figures, no digit at all: `· title` — naming the article's interpretation as one among others — then `· claim1`/`· verifier1`/`· ouvre1`, same for 2, 3 and 4; each claim is a question ending in « ? », `verifier` an infinitive sentence saying how to validate or invalidate its assumption (no source: those belong to slide 5); `ouvre`, not drawn, is the pair of readings the blind spot leaves undecided, e.g. `1,3`, and the four pairs must together cover the three readings), `lectures` (`· title`, `· lecture1`…`· lecture3`, the title a question about the alternative to the article's reading, each `nom | hypothèse | chiffre à suivre`, the names equal to the axe's alternatives, the last part starting with the measure in bold (`**…**`) and saying « validé si … », no digit), `hook`; `writing` runs without a pause. A figure is `valeur | libellé | comparaison` (the value ≤9 characters), the slide 2 note `remarque | texte` or `source | texte`: keep that shape when the author rewrites one. `fix` fields are `<step>.<field>` (`axe.lecture2`, `chiffres.fig1`, `recit.row2`, `angle_mort.ouvre1`, `lectures.lecture3`, `hook.title`…) and `caption`. If the author renames an alternative, rename it in both `axe` and `lectures`, or the ⚠ on `lectures` returns. Slides 2–3 stay in the article's frame (a recomputed figure must say « calculé »; a quoted passage is copied word for word); slides 4–5 rest on the model's general knowledge: tell the author the clues there are unverified pointers, and that the three alternatives are our grid, not the article's. If the article offers no explanation, `recit` holds only data: relay that ⚠ rather than inventing rows. Every rendered field has a character cap that keeps it on its slide: relay the ⚠ that `choose` prints rather than trimming silently. `check` does not cover this format.
