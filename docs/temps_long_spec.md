# TEMPS LONG — specification

The TEMPS LONG archetype: the full description of the slides, the axe and its moves, the guided steps, the flat option fields, the rules and flags. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the summary; the author-facing description is `docs/carousels_archetypes.md`; the prompts are in `agent/prompts/temps_long_steps/` (their caps are written `{{cap:…}}` and filled from `agent/temps_long_adapt_agent.py`). Moved from `CLAUDE.md` without rewriting.

## Purpose and slides

`temps_long` — the TEMPS LONG archetype (`docs/carousels_archetypes.md`): use it when the perspective of time is particularly helpful to analyse a position — the media or its author asserts a novelty, a trend, a drift or a return, and dated reference points (episodes, texts) can test it; without such an assertion it is a poor fit. 7 slides in the optimized deck's visual language. The text below describes the default order and the `forme` move slide by slide, then the axe, the moves and the `seuil` move that the first real deck uses (it reads position, lecture, episodes, tournants, pièges); one word per idea runs through the deck: *critères* (what each camp applies), *signes* (the second camp's), *étiquettes* (the marks on the timelines, « pills » in the code), *ligne* (the limit the question looks for). Hook (identical to the optimized one; the title is the raw fact, in the media's words and attributed (the pill carries the date), the promise « Prendre du recul sur … ») → La position (slide 2, in the article's own frame: the slide reads at two levels, as slides 3 and 4: the `title` is the claim (≤46 chars, two lines at 70 px, centred, a key passage in gold `**…**` required) and the `lead` the sentence under it that names the author (≤90 chars, two lines at 34 px, saying who the author is as the article does); under « Le détail », four rows in two columns (≤108 chars each at 24 px grey, one idea each in plain words, 1–2 key passages in gold), a `kicker` above (≤30 chars, who speaks; empty = « Ce que dit l'auteur »), each row with its own heading `ecarte_label`… ≤30 chars specific to the article — empty = the generic « Ce qu'il écarte » / « Son diagnostic » / « Pourquoi ça a duré, et pourquoi ça casse » / « Sa conclusion »): what he sets aside, his diagnosis, the mechanism, his conclusion; everything comes from the text, quotes verbatim, a figure only if the source gives it; `position` is optional — an older deck without it keeps 6 slides and the numbers below shift by one) → Les épisodes récents (slide 3: 4–5 events of the last ten years that people remember most on the subject — wide coverage AND a documented trace — each with a one-line remark saying what happened, different on every row and never repeating the label's words; the lead quotes a media word) → Les tournants (slide 4: the laws, doctrine texts and public statements that changed the rules over 30–50 years, reaching back at least 25 (10 for the `seuil` move), 3–4 points, the latest from the last three years — a bill under discussion included, with its status — each with a two-line remark saying what the text changed and what it could change, each thesis attributed to its camp) → La lecture du temps (slide 5, the critical-thinking slide: the lead says how to decide and on what (« Pour trancher : comparer … d'un épisode à l'autre »), on ONE measure; three cards rupture / continuité / cycle, each `hypothèse ? | test` (the third part, `où regarder`, is optional once `lecture.where` is set) — the card's first line is its pictogram (a step, a slope, a wave: no data), its name and its question, e.g. « Rupture : Le déficit change-t-il de cap en 2026 ? » (a break of the trajectory, never a record level: a record is what a rising trend also gives) (the question is worded so that a « yes » supports its own reading: continuity says PROLONGEMENT — « Le déficit suit-il son cours ? », « Octobre prolonge-t-il la tendance ? » — never « comme avant » nor « les textes changent-ils … ? »; the texts of slide 4 enter the continuity test only when the article itself says they changed nothing), the test (≤100 chars) carries one **bold** passage that tells the reading apart from the other two (the trajectory or the jump, the prolongation, the return — never the shared measure); the lead (≤140 chars, three lines at most) is the paragraph that links the slide to the two timelines: one sentence in the imperative names the measure and the possible explanation (« Pour trancher : comparez les interpellations par manifestant d'un épisode à l'autre, et voyez si les lois peuvent l'expliquer », never « sur la même mesure »; the episodes of slide 3 are the points where it is read, and the repères of slide 4 are named with their own word — « les lois », « les règles budgétaires » — never « les textes »; they are a possible explanation of the trend, never an established one), so the cards stay generic and do not repeat the link; and ONE line under it, with a lens icon, says where to find the series all three readings are tested on (`lecture.where`, ≤48 chars, an organisation, never a URL; empty = each card carries its own, as in older decks); the three tests must give different results on the same series; the closing question — « Votre intuition avant de regarder : rupture, continuité ou cycle ? » — is not drawn here) → Les pièges de la lecture (slide 6: four traps of a time series, **none imposed, each resting on a sentence of the article** (a generic trap — « en volume ou en proportion » — is dropped: the prompt asks which sentence it tests) — the starting point, « après n'est pas à cause de » (a serious candidate when the thesis states a cause), the episodes we remember, visibility, regression to the mean, a change of measure… — title and lead say in plain words what the slide is for (« Quatre points à vérifier avant de comparer » — the title is the only header, there is no sub-line), each trap is a question on slide 5's measure) → CTA (identical to the optimized one; its « Commentez — … » line takes `lecture.question`).

## The axe and the moves

Step `axe` (numbered 0, so the other step files keep their names) comes first and is never rendered: it fixes the hook's title, the raw attributed fact (`fait`, ≤64 characters, quotes verbatim, one key word in gold), the question the deck tests (`title`, ≤100 characters, a question that takes a quoted word of the media — it becomes the hook's promise), the **move** that answers it (`forme`, `seuil`, `sequence`, `nouveaute`, `delai`, `memoire`), the media's word it tests (`mot`, found in the source), what the deck gives as the answer (`reponse`) and, for `forme` and `seuil`, the three card names of the lecture slide (`criteres`, ≤10 characters each: « Rupture, Continuité, Cycle », or e.g. « Cible, Proportion, Effet »). The guided menu offers a different move per option, with a reason and a ★; every later step receives it as fixed context. The lecture slide is one generic layout — a lead, an optional line under it (`where_label : where`), three cards `nom | question ? | texte` (name ≤10, question ≤40 ending with « ? », text ≤100 with one **bold** passage), an optional `author` line (≤60) and a `kicker` (≤30) — and the move only changes what the prompt asks for: *forme* = the three readings below; *seuil* = a decision table, then the second voice's signs: the table's two columns carry the pair of words the first camp uses itself in the media (`head1` / `head2`, ≤20 characters: « Approprié » | « Excessif », from « ne sont pas des réponses appropriées » and « recours excessif ») — the hook title asks our question, the table reports their words, nothing is read into them, each of its three rows one of the first camp's criteria as a regular card (`card1`…`card3`, each `critère | question | première issue | seconde issue`: the criterion as the title with its icon, a question that says what the test looks at as a complement, ≤28 characters — « Qui est visé ? », « Par rapport aux violences ? » —, and two outcomes ≤30 characters each, in the camp's own words or their direct opposite — never a reading of ours such as « manifestants pacifiques » when the camp does not say it —, no colour connotation); under it a paragraph that is the lead's twin — same type and width, same « Pour <camp>, … » form (`aside_heading`, ≤60, e.g. « Pour Amnesty, sept signes d'un glissement autoritaire. » — no « » when the words are the media's own, not the camp's) — and the second camp's signs as 2 to 7 small cards (`aside1`…`aside7`, each `nom | phrase | icône`: a short name ≤10 characters in the order of the axe's `criteres2`, the explicit phrase as the media words it ≤34, an optional icon key) The names are the PILLS of the timelines: a point may carry up to 2 tags (`date | repère | remarque | critère, signe`) and a timeline has a `layout` (`liste` by default; `annees` — the year drawn large, its month and range end small beside it, used for the episodes of the first real deck; `axe` — a centred spine, texts alternating from side to side, the gap in years between two texts written on the spine, used for its tournants; the tournants label is ≤50 and its remark ≤56 characters because `axe` gives a text half the slide) and may carry an optional `key` (≤100 characters, under the rows, empty by default), the tags drawn under its date — all gold, whichever camp's criterion it is, each with its icon — and a pill says what an event bears on, never which column it falls in. The two camps do not use the same kind of criteria (tests with outcomes vs signs of a drift), so the lead (≤100 characters) says what the table gives and the columns are not aligned. No line under the lead nor author line (`where`, `author` stay empty); *sequence* / *delai* = episode → next listed text and the gap, dates only; *nouveaute* = three dimensions on which « new » could be argued; *memoire* = why three episodes are remembered. The hook has its own `tagline` (≤110, two lines at most, names the camps, key words in gold; empty = « Des épisodes, des textes, trois lectures pour **en juger**. »). A move may set the order of the middle slides (`presentation.order`, from `_MOVE_ORDER`): `seuil` reads position, lecture, episodes, tournants, pièges; the others keep position, episodes, tournants, lecture, pièges. The detailed description of slide 5 below is the `forme` move.

## The guided steps

Seven guided steps (`agent/temps_long_adapt_agent.py`, prompts in `agent/prompts/temps_long_steps/`): `axe` first, then `position` (the author's stand, shown as slide 2 and written before the timelines and the lecture slide, which lean on it), then `tournants` (the noyau), `episodes`, `lecture`, `pieges`, `hook` (its `promise` and `tagline`; its title is the axe's `fait`); `writing` (the caption) follows without a pause. Older decks that wrote `palier` / `vague_de_fond` still load.

## How the cascade runs

The write order depends on the move the axe fixes (`_WRITE_ORDER`, `steps_in_order()`): `seuil` writes axe, position, **lecture**, episodes, tournants, pieges, hook — the table and the signs come BEFORE the timelines, whose rows are then chosen to bear on its criteria and signs (an advisory flag on the second timeline lists the criteria and signs no row is tagged with); the other moves keep axe, position, tournants, episodes, lecture, pieges, hook. Each step's message carries an « ORDRE D'ÉCRITURE » note (what is written, what comes after). Step files keep their number (`3_episodes.json`) whatever the order; the stamp chain follows the order. The prompts never type a character cap: they write `{{cap:tournants.label}}` and `fill_caps()` fills it from `_CAPS` and the constants (`prompt_caps()`), a test refuses a literal « ≤N ». `agent/prompts/temps_long_steps/examples/<n>_<step>.md` holds the author's past decisions (`## Retenus` / `## Écartés (et pourquoi)`), loaded into the message of an option step. The pipeline sets the timelines' layouts (`annees` for the episodes, `axe` for the texts, `_LAYOUTS`); a deck edits them in its JSON. After a pick on `tournants` or `episodes`, `choose` checks each point against the web (`agent/temps_long_verify.py`, the repo's claim verifier: news and public databases, a stance per source; not with `--no-api`, which has no API key): a point with no supporting source or a contradicted one comes back as ⚠ (advisory), the results are kept in `<n>_<step>.verification.json` and the choice log, and shown nowhere on a slide. The search is built for news, so « no source found » can mean a law covered only on Légifrance. `python main.py check <extract.json>` on a temps_long deck runs the deck-level check (`agent/temps_long_deck_check.py`, no model call): hard — a tag that names no criterion or sign, a timeline under 3 points, two traps with one title; advisory — criteria or signs never tagged, a tagline naming no camp, tags without their note, two leads sharing their words, a traps title ending on « avant de trancher/classer » alone, and the points the web check did not confirm. Prefer `fix` to `choose --edit` for wording on an early step: an edit through `choose` re-runs the later steps, `fix` only re-stamps them.

## Options, fields and rules

Same semantics as the other guided runs — options with a reason and a ★, `choose --pick/--edit/--more`, `fix <fmt_dir> <step>.<field>` (`episodes.rep1`, `lecture.card3` (`lecture.cycle` in an older deck), `pieges.piege1`, `position.lead`, `axe.fait`, `hook.promise`…) or `caption`, choice log in `choices.jsonl`; `fix` re-stamps later steps without regenerating and prints no warnings. Re-run `produce` with the same metadata flags (`--title`, `--source`, `--url`, `--published-at`): they feed the step hash, and without them the steps are retired. Flat option strings: a reference point `date | repère | remarque` (`rep1`…`rep5`, the date ≤14 characters and holding a year), a card `nom | question ? | texte` (`card1`…`card3`: name ≤10 — equal to the axe's `criteres`, in order, when it has some —, question ≤36 and ending in « ? », text ≤100 with at least two `**`; an older deck's reading `hypothèse ? | test [| où regarder]` under `rupture` / `continuite` / `cycle` (where ≤44 and no URL, optional once `where` is set) is still read and checked), a trap `titre | texte` (`piege1`…`piege4`; the text, ≤92 characters, is a question or a watch-out resting on a fact of the article). Everything outside the quoted lead word is the model's general knowledge and unverified: dates only, **never a trend figure** (a `%`, an amount or a multiple is a hard error, except on `position`, which follows the source), organisations named with acronyms spelled out and never a URL, and a reference point is something to examine, never an equivalent or a cause. Hard rules: every text field is capped in characters to fit its lines whatever the words (the caps live in `agent/temps_long_adapt_agent.py`, were measured on the rendered slides with random texts, and a seeded worst-case render test guards them), point, reading and trap formats, no trend figure, no accusing word outside « », the lead of slides 3–4 quotes a media word found in the source, questions that end in « ? », 4 hashtags. They repair a generated option and come back as ⚠ on the author's pick; advisory flags: a point with no remark, a remark that echoes the lead's quoted word, repeats another remark's or repeats its own label, a reading test that refers to nothing of the lead or of slides 3–4, an episode that looks like a point of the tournants (same event listed twice — a shared year alone is not flagged), an episodes slide reaching back more than 12 years or a tournants slide that does not reach 25 or has no text from the last 3 years (measured from the latest year in the source), a trap that cites nothing of slide 5's measure.

## Images and output

One picture opens and closes the deck: `background.*`, else `image.*` / `cover.*`, full-bleed on slide 1 and darkened on slide 7 (the other formats still ignore `image.*` as a background; `background_image(…, or_cover=True)`). `python main.py check <extract.json>` runs the deck-level check (see « How the cascade runs »); `--reader` is not available. Output folder: `<stem>/temps_long/`.
